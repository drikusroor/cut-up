        Voice
        Singer

        The words are sounded out from their spelling and sung through three bandpass filters, which is what a vowel is. Vocoder and talk box run that speech through a real fourteen-band vocoder with the melody as its carrier. Lines land between the rests in the tune, so a melody with room to breathe sings better than one with none.

        Melody

        Drag a note up and down to change its pitch, left and right to move it in time; drag its right-hand edge to make it longer or shorter. Double-click empty space to add a note, alt-click or right-click one to remove it. Hand edits are kept as deviations from the generated line: they show up green over the dashed ghost of where the note started, and “Reset to generated” gives you the original back. Grey lines are bars; the highlighted row is the key's root. The blue notes along the bottom are the bass — it is written against the drums and the melody rather than by hand, so roll it rather than dragging it.

        🎛 Mix

        The desk

        Five channels — the tune, the voice on it, the chords, the bass and the kit — each with a fader, a pan, a three-band EQ, a squeeze and a send to one shared plate. Everything moves while the song is playing, and the mix travels with the song: an exported file comes out the way you left it here.

        The transport's part switches above leave a part out of the arrangement altogether; muting it here only turns it down. Soloing is the quickest way to hear what one instrument is actually doing.
        🫀 Feel

        Humanize

        Nobody plays on the grid. This pulls every note a little off its step — and lets each player be off it in their own way: a drummer who rushes the hats, a bass that sits behind the beat. At zero it is a machine again.
        The deviations are seeded like everything else here, so the same seed is the same take — and the MIDI export is the take you just heard rather than a second, differently sloppy one.
        🎼 Tuning

        Temperament

        Out of tune

        Detune is a fixed offset per instrument — a section that tuned five minutes ago. Drift is per note, which is a singer rather than a piano. Both come off the seed, so a piece keeps the same slightly-wrong tuning every time you play it.
        Teach it what you like

        It deals two or three sections out of the composer's own deck. Listen, say what you think, and it deals again. Nothing here is a quiz with right answers — it is only ever learning your opinion, and the only way it can be wrong is by disagreeing with you.

        1–5 answers the highlighted question and moves on, Space plays whatever that question is about, Enter saves the hand and deals the next. Leave anything you have no view on unanswered — a shrug is not a score, and it is better for the model to hear nothing than to hear a guess.

        The model

        Scaled by how well the model did on rounds it was never trained on, so one that has not beaten a coin has no say at whatever this is set to. Run npm run taste instead of npm start and every answer is written straight to data/taste.jsonl; training then rewrites models/taste.json, and both are yours to commit.
